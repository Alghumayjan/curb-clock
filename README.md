# Curb Clock

Live street-parking guide for Manhattan from the Battery to 130th St, river to river: Lower Manhattan, the Villages, SoHo, Chelsea, Midtown, the Upper East and West Sides, Morningside Heights, Harlem and East Harlem.

**Open it on your phone:** https://alghumayjan.github.io/curb-clock/

- **Map:** where you can legally park by day and hour, read from NYC DOT's parking-sign records. Green is free, blue is metered, dashed yellow means you'd have to move before your stay ends, thin red is no parking.
- **Search:** type where and when, like "Spring & Mercer at 8pm", "80 Spring St for 2 hrs", "W 125th & Lenox" or a landmark ("Apollo", "Columbia", "Union Square"). Any two crossing streets or a house number works anywhere on the map.
- **Street-cleaning spots:** pick a window under **Street cleaning** (or type "9-10:30 cleaning near 72nd & Lex friday") and the map shows only the curbs swept in that window on that day. Everyone who moved for the sweeper comes back as it ends, so the time jumps to the end of the window and each spot shows how long you can stay after it (usually days, until the next cleaning). The menu lists the windows near your destination first, with how many spaces each frees up.
- **Drive mode:** tap **Guide me to free parking**, then **Use my GPS**. It follows your location, tells you whether the block you're on has free curb on a side you can pull into, and speaks the turns to the nearest free block along the one-way streets. **No spot here** moves you on to the next free block. With a cleaning window picked, it guides you to those curbs.

Data: NYC Open Data — Parking Regulation Locations and Signs (59,926 signs on 9,098 block faces, updated Nov 20, 2026), street centerlines and address ranges, fire hydrants, and ParkNYC meter rates. Holiday suspensions are loaded through Jan 6, 2027.

Everything at 60th St and below is inside Manhattan's congestion pricing zone, so driving in is tolled. Stretches of curb with no recorded sign show as "no sign data" rather than free.

Zone edges come from the city's distance-from-corner measurements and can be off by a car length. Driveways and temporary construction signs aren't in the data, and GPS between tall buildings can lag a block. Always read the posted signs, set the app up before you drive, and keep your eyes on the road.
